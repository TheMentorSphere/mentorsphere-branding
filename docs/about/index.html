About The MentorSphere

More than tutoring, without losing the personal relationship

The MentorSphere was created to bring learning, mentoring and education support together. It is designed for people whose needs do not stay inside one subject, one diagnosis or one stage of life.

Meet Luke Turner

Teaching experience shaped by very different classrooms

I have more than ten years of teaching experience across mainstream schools, specialist provisions, prison education and one-to-one support. Those settings taught me that the same explanation, environment or expectation will not work for every learner.

I created The MentorSphere to offer a more flexible model: one that can teach curriculum knowledge while also paying attention to confidence, executive function, communication and the practical realities surrounding education.

I have ADHD myself, and lived experience informs the way I understand inconsistency, overwhelm, motivation and the importance of feeling genuinely understood. Lived insight supports the work, but it sits alongside clear boundaries, safeguarding and ongoing professional development.

Support should not feel like a patchwork of disconnected services.

The goal is one coherent relationship that can adapt when the learner's priorities change.

Why the business exists

People are often forced to retell their story before anyone can help

For neurodivergent learners and families, the administrative and emotional cost of seeking support can become a barrier in itself. Forms, new professionals, repeated histories and disconnected advice all use energy that may already be limited.

The MentorSphere aims to reduce that repetition. A person may begin with tutoring, then need ADHD coaching, study planning or SEND support. The service can change while the understanding, communication style and human relationship remain consistent.

Core values

What stays consistent across every branch

The subject or service may change. These principles should not.

A

Accessibility

Communication, pace, sensory load and session structure can be adapted to reduce unnecessary barriers.

P

Personalisation

Support is built around strengths, needs, interests, goals and circumstances rather than a standard package.

N

Neuro-affirming practice

Neurodiversity is treated as natural human variation, while real barriers and support needs are still taken seriously.

E

Emotional safety

Mistakes, difficult feelings and changing capacity are met with calmness, honesty and clear boundaries.

M

Meaningful learning

Knowledge is linked to real interests, future goals and practical contexts wherever possible.

C

Continuity

Good internal notes and communication help the learner feel recognised rather than treated as a new file each time.

Who The MentorSphere works with

Specialist support, open to a wide range of learners

The MentorSphere specialises in neurodivergent learners and those with additional needs, but a diagnosis is not a condition of being welcomed. Learners may need help because of disrupted education, anxiety, low confidence, gaps in knowledge, home education, exam pressure or simply a poor fit with conventional teaching.

The first conversation focuses on the person and the goal, then considers which branch or combination of services is most appropriate.

  • Children and young people from KS1 to KS4
  • 11+ and Functional Skills learners
  • Home-educated and EOTAS learners
  • Adults returning to learning
  • People with diagnosed or suspected ADHD
  • Parents seeking practical support
  • Families navigating SEND or exam systems

Longer-term direction

  • More low-cost digital resources for families and learners
  • Group programmes and practical workshops
  • CPD and neurodiversity training for education settings
  • Funded or subsidised support through a future access fund
  • A digital hub for resources, community and ongoing guidance

Growing without losing the human feel

Wider access, with personal care protected

The long-term aim is to reach more families through resources, groups and carefully chosen partnerships. Growth should not mean becoming impersonal or reducing people to a short set of notes.

Clear communication, continuity and respect will remain central to how the service develops.
