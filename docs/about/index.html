About The MentorSphere

Joined-up support across learning, neurodiversity and education

The MentorSphere was created to bring tutoring, coaching and education/SEND support into one consistent service for people whose needs do not fit neatly within one subject, diagnosis or stage of life.

Meet Luke Turner

More than ten years working across education and SEND settings

I have more than ten years' experience working across education and SEND, including mainstream schools, specialist provisions, prison education and one-to-one support. These settings showed me that effective support cannot be reduced to one teaching method, one subject or one label.

I created The MentorSphere to bring learning, coaching and education support together in a flexible model that considers confidence, executive function, communication and the wider realities surrounding education.

I have ADHD, and lived experience informs the way I understand inconsistency, overwhelm, motivation and the importance of feeling genuinely understood. That insight supports the work alongside clear boundaries, safeguarding and ongoing professional development.

Support should not feel like a patchwork of disconnected services.

The goal is one coherent relationship that can adapt when a person's priorities change.

Why the business exists

People are often forced to retell their story before anyone can help

For neurodivergent people and families, the administrative and emotional cost of seeking support can become a barrier in itself. Forms, new professionals, repeated histories and disconnected advice all use energy that may already be limited.

The MentorSphere aims to reduce that repetition. A person may begin with tutoring, ADHD coaching or education/SEND support, then move between services as needs change. The understanding, communication style and human relationship can remain consistent.

Core values

What stays consistent across every branch

The subject or service may change. These principles should not.

A

Accessibility

Communication, pace, sensory load and session structure can be adapted to reduce unnecessary barriers.

P

Personalisation

Support is built around strengths, needs, interests, goals and circumstances rather than a standard package.

N

Neuro-affirming practice

Neurodiversity is treated as natural human variation, while real barriers and support needs are still taken seriously.

E

Emotional safety

Mistakes, difficult feelings and changing capacity are met with calmness, honesty and clear boundaries.

M

Meaningful learning

Knowledge is linked to real interests, future goals and practical contexts wherever possible.

C

Continuity

Good internal notes and communication help the learner feel recognised rather than treated as a new file each time.

Who The MentorSphere works with

Specialist support, open to a wide range of people

The MentorSphere specialises in neurodivergent people and those with additional needs, but a diagnosis is not a condition of being welcomed. Someone may need help because of disrupted education, anxiety, low confidence, gaps in knowledge, home education, exam pressure or simply a poor fit with conventional teaching.

The first conversation focuses on the person and the goal, then considers which branch or combination of services is most appropriate.

  • Children and young people from KS1 to KS4
  • 11+ and Functional Skills learners
  • Home-educated and EOTAS learners
  • Adults returning to learning
  • People with diagnosed or suspected ADHD
  • Parents seeking practical support
  • Families navigating SEND or exam systems

Principles for future growth

  • Widen access without reducing personal care
  • Keep communication clear and accessible
  • Protect continuity as the service develops
  • Be honest about scope, boundaries and capacity

Growing without losing the human feel

Wider access, with personal care protected

The long-term aim is to reach more families through resources, groups and carefully chosen partnerships. Growth should not mean becoming impersonal or reducing people to a short set of notes.

Clear communication, continuity and respect will remain central to how the service develops.
