Education outside a standard school setting

Home education and EOTAS support

Practical support with curriculum planning, realistic timetables, progress evidence and a joined-up approach shaped around the learner.

Discuss education planning See what support can include

Elective home education and EOTAS are different routes. Support is tailored to the learner's actual context and does not replace legal or statutory advice.

Who it may help

Planning that fits the learner's needs and current capacity

This service may help home-educated and EOTAS learners, or families trying to organise education outside a standard school setting.

The focus is practical: creating a workable plan, identifying priorities and recording progress without forcing the learner into a standard template.

Possible starting points

  • A curriculum that feels too broad or unclear
  • A timetable that is difficult to sustain
  • Gaps in progress evidence or records
  • Several professionals or services working separately
  • Preparation for exams or a future transition

What support can include

A realistic plan rather than a school day copied at home

Curriculum and priorities

Clarify learning goals, essential subjects, interests, wider development and the areas that need attention first.

Timetables and routines

Build a flexible weekly structure around energy, executive function, sensory needs, appointments and current capacity.

Evidence and coordination

Organise progress examples, learning records, next steps and agreed information for relevant professionals or services.

Useful information to bring

  • The learner's strengths, interests and goals
  • Current learning activities or provision
  • Relevant reports or support plans
  • Examples of work or progress already recorded
  • Current routines, energy patterns and barriers
  • Important dates, exams or transition plans

It is fine if these are incomplete. The support can begin by deciding what information would be useful to collect.

Joined-up planning

Connect learning, wellbeing and practical reality

Education planning works best when it considers academic goals alongside confidence, communication, executive function and the wider circumstances affecting learning.

Where appropriate consent is in place, support can help organise agreed information across family members, tutors and relevant education professionals.

Pricing

Quoted around the planning required

The initial discussion is free. A defined planning task is quoted in advance, while a package involving several stages or documents receives a bespoke quotation.

Complexity, deadlines, document volume and the expected output affect the fee. The scope and price are agreed before substantive paid work begins.

What the service cannot guarantee

The MentorSphere cannot guarantee local authority decisions, funding, provision, placements, exam outcomes or acceptance of particular evidence.

The service provides education planning and preparation. It does not provide legal representation, legal advice, clinical diagnosis or medical advice.

Common questions

Before planning support begins

Are home education and EOTAS the same?

No. They are different education routes with different contexts and responsibilities. The introductory discussion identifies which situation applies so practical support can be scoped appropriately.

Does the plan need to copy a school timetable?

No standard template is imposed. Planning is shaped around the learner's goals, strengths, needs, routines and current capacity.

Can support include tutoring?

Yes, where tutoring is useful and agreed. Learning and tutoring is a separate pathway, but it can form part of a joined-up plan without requiring the learner to start again.

Can you guarantee funding or local authority agreement?

No. The service can help organise plans and evidence, but statutory and funding decisions remain with the relevant body.
