Home Education Support

Build a home education approach that works in real life

Personalised help for families considering, starting or already navigating home education, shaped around the learner's strengths, needs, interests, goals and current capacity.

Discuss home education support Explore support

The Home Education Mentor is a supporting visual identity within The MentorSphere, not a separate company or service provider.

Who it supports

Start with the learner and the family

Support may help families considering elective home education, families in the first weeks or months, and established home-educating families whose current approach feels stuck.

It can also support neurodivergent learners who need a different approach, older learners approaching qualifications or transitions, and families considering a possible return to school.

Some families search for home schooling or neurodivergent home education. This site uses Home Education as the normal public term.

Planning reflects real life

  • Learner strengths, needs, interests and goals
  • Emotional and sensory needs
  • Current capacity and family circumstances
  • Available time and realistic budgets

Home education is not assumed to be the right answer to every school difficulty. The first step is neutral fit and triage.

How support works

A flexible cycle that changes when needs change

1

Fit and triage

Clarify whether the need concerns elective home education, EOTAS, school attendance, tutoring, SEND support or a mixture. Identify urgent deadlines and useful free routes.

2

Discovery

Gather family goals, learner voice, educational history, strengths, barriers, capacity, resources and current concerns.

3

Starting-point review

Use existing work, light-touch assessment or observation where appropriate, avoiding testing that would add distress or disengagement.

4

Personalised plan

Agree priorities, curriculum approach, weekly rhythm, resources, responsibilities, progress evidence and review points.

5

Implementation support

Use agreed combinations of parent strategy, learner mentoring, subject tutoring, coordination and review.

6

Review and progression

Update what is not working and prepare for qualifications, college, training, employment, reintegration or another pathway.

Home education principles

Flexible, neutral and centred on the learner's voice

Support can use structured, learner-led or blended approaches. It should reflect family capacity, include rest, regulation and real-life learning, and avoid recreating school unnecessarily.

The plan is reviewed when it stops working. Tutoring, ADHD coaching and Education & SEND Support can be coordinated where useful.

Service boundaries

Parents remain responsible for providing a suitable education when they electively home educate. The MentorSphere can support planning, delivery choices and records, but does not take over that responsibility.

The service does not provide legal advice, tell a parent whether to deregister, act as a statutory inspection service, guarantee acceptance of a plan or evidence format, replace clinical support, provide crisis support or include unlimited contact.

Joined-up support

Use the right service without starting again

Families can use more than one MentorSphere service without repeating their full onboarding story. The shared relationship and agreed information can move with the support.

Direct subject teaching

Maths, English, science and exam preparation remain part of Tutoring.

ADHD-specific coaching

Executive function, routines and identity-focused coaching sit within ADHD Coaching.

Statutory and access contexts

EHCP, EOTAS and contested education processes sit within Education & SEND Support. Seek appropriate legally based advice where needed.
