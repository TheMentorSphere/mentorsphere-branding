Areas of support

Reading, writing and understanding how language works

Lessons can strengthen core literacy, respond to current schoolwork or prepare for 11+ and GCSE English Language. Skills are taught in a connected way so that reading can inform writing and vocabulary can support clearer expression.

Exam preparation includes understanding questions, selecting evidence, explaining effects, planning responses and using time effectively.

Support may include

  • Reading fluency and comprehension
  • Creative, persuasive and transactional writing
  • Vocabulary, spelling, punctuation and grammar
  • Language analysis and use of evidence
  • Planning, editing and communicating ideas clearly
  • 11+ and GCSE English Language preparation

Meaningful writing

Real audiences and interests can give writing a purpose

The required skill stays central, but the topic and format can be chosen to create a more useful reason to read, discuss and write.

1

Interest-led texts

Articles, stories and examples linked to a learner’s interests can create a stronger route into comprehension and analysis.

2

Real-world formats

Reviews, applications, speeches, reports or journalism can connect writing choices to careers and everyday communication.

3

Ideas before accuracy

Talking, mapping or recording ideas first can reduce the load of generating content and writing accurately at the same time.

Adaptations may include

  • Verbal planning before written work
  • Chunked texts and uncluttered page layouts
  • Vocabulary banks, prompts and model answers
  • Typing, speech-to-text or shared writing where appropriate
  • Flexible pace, processing time and short breaks

Flexible and neurodiversity-affirming

Make the task clearer without making ideas smaller

Adaptations can reduce working-memory, handwriting or processing demands while keeping the intellectual goal meaningful. Communication preferences and current capacity are part of lesson planning.

A diagnosis is not required. The focus is on identifying practical barriers and helping the learner express what they know.

Common questions

English tutoring FAQs

Do you offer GCSE English Literature?

The advertised GCSE service is English Language. Literature skills may overlap in some lessons, but support for a specific Literature text or course should be discussed before booking.

Can lessons help a reluctant writer?

Yes. Lessons can separate idea generation from recording, use motivating formats and build the task in smaller stages while keeping the learner’s own voice central.

Can English be combined with another subject?

Yes. A plan can alternate subjects or change focus around current priorities. The structure and price are agreed clearly.
