Areas of support

Core skills, problem-solving and exam preparation

Lessons can focus on current curriculum work, rebuild earlier foundations or prepare for a specific qualification. The starting point is what the learner understands now, not only the year group written on a timetable.

Exam preparation combines mathematical understanding with question interpretation, working clearly, checking methods and using time well.

Topics may include

  • Number, calculation and proportional reasoning
  • Fractions, decimals, percentages and ratio
  • Algebra, sequences and graphs
  • Shape, space, measure and geometry
  • Statistics, probability and data
  • Problem-solving and everyday maths

Personalisation in practice

A route into maths that makes sense to the learner

The curriculum goal stays clear while examples, pacing and ways of recording can change.

1

Relevant examples

Statistics from sport, probability in games, budgeting or map scale can make abstract ideas more concrete.

2

Visible thinking

Worked examples, diagrams and colour-coded steps can reduce the load of holding a whole method in mind.

3

Confidence rebuilding

Earlier gaps can be addressed without judgement, with enough repetition to make a method feel secure.

Adaptations may include

  • Smaller steps and predictable lesson routines
  • Reduced visual clutter and clear checkpoints
  • Verbal explanation before written recording
  • Flexible pace, movement and short breaks
  • Choice over examples and ways to demonstrate learning

Neurodiversity-affirming tutoring

Adapt the lesson, not the learner’s identity

Support can respond to working-memory load, attention, anxiety, processing time and communication preferences. A diagnosis is not required for teaching to be flexible and respectful.

Strengths and interests are used as routes into learning, while real barriers and support needs are taken seriously.

Common questions

Maths tutoring FAQs

Which post-16 maths topics can be supported?

Selected post-16 topics may be available by agreement. Please share the course, topic and current goal so suitability can be confirmed before booking.

Can tutoring help when there are gaps from disrupted education?

Yes. A starting-point assessment can identify useful foundations, then lessons can connect those gaps to current goals without making the learner repeat everything.

Can maths be combined with another subject?

Yes. A plan can alternate subjects or change focus around current priorities. The structure and price are agreed clearly.
