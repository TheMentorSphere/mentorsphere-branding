Personalisation in practice

The lesson is adapted before the learner is asked to adapt

Traditional tutoring can repeat the same explanation more slowly and call it differentiation. The MentorSphere takes a wider view: the example, format, pace, level of challenge and way of responding can all change.

A football fan might compare player statistics. A gamer might calculate damage, probability or map scale. A future journalist might build persuasive writing around a live issue. The curriculum goal stays clear, but the route towards it becomes more relevant.

Learn about the mentoring-led approach

Adaptations may include

  • Visual models and worked examples
  • Smaller steps with clear checkpoints
  • Movement, short breaks and flexible pacing
  • Verbal-first explanation before written recording
  • Reduced visual clutter and consistent lesson routines
  • Choice over topics, examples and ways to demonstrate learning
  • Extra reassurance after mistakes or difficult tasks

Common questions

Before you book

Do you only tutor neurodivergent learners?

No. Neurodiversity-affirming teaching is a specialism, but the service is open to learners from all backgrounds. Flexible, respectful teaching benefits everyone.

Does the learner need to keep their camera on?

A camera can help communication, but it is not automatically required throughout every session. Camera use can be adapted around comfort, sensory needs and safeguarding arrangements.

Can one learner study more than one subject?

Yes. A plan can combine subjects, alternate them across weeks or change focus around exams and current priorities.

Can lessons support home education or EOTAS?

Yes. Lessons can sit within a wider home education, EHE or EOTAS plan, including bespoke curriculum and progress evidence where agreed.

What technology is needed?

A laptop, desktop or tablet with a stable internet connection is recommended. Lessonspace works in a modern browser and does not require a download. A touchscreen or stylus is useful but optional.
