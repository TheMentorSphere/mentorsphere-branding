Supporting young people works best together

When coaching a young person, progress is often strongest when parents or guardians are involved alongside the young person's individual sessions.

The exact balance will be agreed case by case and will depend on the young person's age, needs, goals and family circumstances. I will usually recommend that around one in every four sessions involves a parent or guardian, either separately or as part of a joint session.

This helps strategies carry over into everyday life while ensuring that the young person's voice, privacy and growing independence remain central to the coaching process.

This is a general recommendation, not a fixed requirement. I will advise on the most appropriate balance following the introductory session and as the coaching relationship develops.

What sessions can cover

Work on the issue underneath the strategy

A planner will not solve a problem if the real barrier is overwhelm, fear of failure, unclear priorities or a routine that relies on perfect motivation. Coaching explores what is actually happening, then builds one practical next step.

There is no fixed curriculum. Goals are reviewed together, and the focus can change as circumstances change.

  • Emotional regulation and recovering after difficult moments
  • Routines, transitions and remembering everyday tasks
  • Planning, prioritising and breaking work into usable steps
  • Motivation, procrastination and task initiation
  • School, university or workplace strategies
  • Relationships, communication and rejection sensitivity
  • Identity, masking, confidence and self-advocacy
  • Preparing for appointments, reviews or support conversations

Workplace support

ADHD coaching through Access to Work

Access to Work is a government scheme that may help fund practical support when ADHD, another disability or a health condition affects someone at work.

Funding may be available for employees, self-employed people and some people who are about to begin or return to paid work. Access to Work will decide what support it is prepared to fund and how much it will contribute.

How to access funding

  1. Check your eligibility through the official GOV.UK Access to Work service.
  2. Apply online or contact the Access to Work helpline.
  3. Explain how ADHD affects you within your work and what support you believe would help.
  4. Request specialist ADHD coaching as part of the support being considered.
  5. Contact The MentorSphere for a written quotation and description of the proposed coaching.
  6. Wait for written confirmation of the approved support before relying on Access to Work to cover the coaching cost.

A formal ADHD diagnosis is not always required to apply, although Access to Work may ask for information about how your condition or difficulties affect you at work.

The MentorSphere can provide a quotation and service outline to support an application, renewal or funding review. However, all funding decisions are made by Access to Work, and coaching should not be assumed to be funded until written approval has been received.

Access to Work-funded ADHD coaching: £110 per 60-minute session.

For employers and organisations

Employer-funded ADHD coaching

Employers and organisations can fund individual ADHD coaching for members of their team.

Coaching is tailored to the employee and may focus on planning and prioritisation, workload management, communication, confidence, emotional regulation, managing distractions and developing sustainable ways of working.

The coaching relationship remains confidential. Information will only be shared with an employer, manager or HR representative where the client has given explicit consent and the information has been agreed in advance.

A space where context matters

Specialist understanding without reducing you to a label

ADHD interacts with identity, relationships, health, recovery and past experiences. These areas can be discussed openly and respectfully where relevant to the coaching goal.

Lived insight supports empathy and practical understanding, but does not replace professional boundaries or appropriate clinical care.

What ADHD coaching is not

Coaching is not psychotherapy, medical treatment, crisis support or a clinical diagnostic assessment. It can sit alongside healthcare, counselling or education support, but it does not replace them. Where a need falls outside coaching, this will be discussed honestly and appropriate signposting may be suggested.

Common questions

Before coaching begins

Do I need a formal ADHD diagnosis?

If you are diagnosed, awaiting assessment or exploring whether ADHD may be relevant, suitability can be discussed during the free discovery call. Coaching focuses on the needs and patterns you describe rather than requiring you to prove them.

Will I receive a written summary after every session?

Sessions use a verbal-first approach to reduce admin and overwhelm. Brief notes are kept internally. Written summaries or reports can be arranged when needed and may carry an additional fee depending on the work involved.

How private are sessions for a young person?

Young people need space to speak honestly. The level of parent or guardian involvement is agreed at the start, while safeguarding concerns are always acted upon. Practical themes and agreed actions can usually be shared without reporting every detail.

Can I keep my camera off?

Camera use can be adapted where appropriate. The priority is a safe and workable session, while also meeting any safeguarding and identification requirements.

Can coaching be funded through Access to Work?

Access to Work coaching may be available where approved within an award. Support can include quotations, session records and agreed reports. Funding must not be assumed until written approval has been received.
