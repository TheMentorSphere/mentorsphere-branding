ADHD coaching with lived insight and practical structure

Neurodiversity-affirming online coaching for adults aged 18+, young people aged 10 to 17, and parents & guardians supporting a young person with ADHD.

The ADHD Mentor is part of one joined-up service alongside learning and tutoring and education and SEND support.

Understand your brain. Harness your strengths.

Coaching that begins with curiosity, not correction

ADHD coaching supports self-understanding, executive function and practical change. Sessions are calm, collaborative and shaped around the person's strengths, needs, communication preferences and current capacity.

A session starts with a low-pressure check-in, agrees a useful focus, explores what is underneath the pattern and finishes with one clear, realistic next step.

The approach is mentoring-led, lived-experience-informed and grounded in more than ten years working across education and SEND settings.

Who ADHD coaching is for

Personalised support at different stages of life

Age 18+

Adults with ADHD

Practical and reflective support for understanding how ADHD affects daily life. Coaching can help with planning, motivation, productivity, emotional regulation, relationships, confidence and building sustainable routines or ways of working.

Ages 10 to 17

Young people with ADHD

Personalised coaching shaped around the young person's strengths, needs, interests and goals. Sessions can explore confidence, emotional regulation, school pressures, routines, growing independence and understanding ADHD without shame. Appropriate safeguarding and consent arrangements are agreed before coaching begins.

Family support

Parents & guardians supporting a young person with ADHD

Practical support to understand a young person's ADHD and respond more effectively. Coaching can explore supportive routines, co-regulation, reducing conflict or overwhelm, compassionate boundaries and growing independence.

Supporting young people works best together

Parent or guardian involvement can help strategies carry over into everyday life. The exact balance is agreed individually, but I will often recommend that around one in every four sessions involves a parent or guardian.

This is a general recommendation rather than a fixed requirement. The young person's voice, privacy and growing independence remain central.

What coaching can support

Work on the issue underneath the strategy

There is no fixed curriculum. Coaching explores what is actually happening, then builds strategies that fit the person's life rather than relying on perfect motivation.

Context matters. Where relevant, sessions can also explore LGBTQIA+ identity, masking, recovery from addiction, disrupted routines, illness, shame and the gap between ability and output.

Lived insight supports empathy and practical understanding, but does not replace professional boundaries or appropriate clinical care.

  • Emotional regulation and recovering after difficult moments
  • Routines, transitions and remembering everyday tasks
  • Planning, prioritising and breaking work into usable steps
  • Motivation, procrastination and task initiation
  • School, university or workplace strategies
  • Relationships, communication and rejection sensitivity
  • Identity, masking, confidence and self-advocacy
  • Preparing for appointments, reviews or support conversations

What ADHD coaching is not

Coaching is not psychotherapy, medical treatment, crisis support or a clinical diagnostic assessment. It can sit alongside healthcare, counselling or education support, but it does not replace them. Where a need falls outside coaching, this will be discussed honestly and appropriate signposting may be suggested.

Common questions

Before coaching begins

Do I need a formal ADHD diagnosis?

If you are diagnosed, awaiting assessment or exploring whether ADHD may be relevant, suitability can be discussed during the free discovery call. Coaching focuses on the needs and patterns you describe rather than requiring you to prove them.

Will I receive a written summary after every session?

Sessions use a verbal-first approach to reduce admin and overwhelm. Brief notes are kept internally. Written summaries or reports can be arranged when needed and may carry an additional fee depending on the work involved.

How private are sessions for a young person?

Young people need space to speak honestly. The level of parent or guardian involvement is agreed at the start, while safeguarding concerns are always acted upon. Practical themes and agreed actions can usually be shared without reporting every detail.

Can I keep my camera off?

Camera use can be adapted where appropriate. The priority is a safe and workable session, while also meeting any safeguarding and identification requirements.
