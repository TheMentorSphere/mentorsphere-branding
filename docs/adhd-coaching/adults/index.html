Everyday life with ADHD

You might recognise some of this

Coaching can be useful when everyday tasks take disproportionate effort. You might recognise one or two of these situations.

Starting takes a deadline
You know the priority, but need urgency to get started. An interesting task can absorb hours while five small admin jobs stay untouched.
Routines are hard to keep
A planner works for a week, then gets forgotten. Bills or appointments slip past, even when they matter to you.
Work or study takes everything
Switching tasks, losing track of time or turning ideas into finished work can make the day unnecessarily exhausting.
Everything needs attention at once
Several competing demands can make it hard to choose a next step, manage frustration or explain what you need.

Make everyday tasks more manageable

Coaching can help you break down tasks, keep track of time or explain your needs. Understanding recurring difficulties matters more than finding a perfect productivity system.

Routines can be useful without working every day. Setbacks offer information about what needs changing, so you can build strategies that fit your life and are easier to use.

How coaching works

A conversation with a practical next step

You choose what to focus on. You do not need a finished plan, and there is no set programme to work through.

  1. Start with what matters now. Choose an issue you would like to work on today.
  2. Understand what gets in the way. Explore the demands, surroundings and patterns that make it difficult.
  3. Choose something realistic to try. Discuss possible approaches together and pick a step that fits your energy and circumstances.
  4. Review and adapt. Talk through what happened when you tried it, then adjust what needs changing.

Practical arrangements

One-to-one sessions take place online across the UK, using an approved platform such as Lessonspace or Google Meet. Paid sessions are 60 minutes; frequency is agreed individually.

We can talk things through without unnecessary written admin. Communication preferences, breaks, camera-off arrangements or shorter sessions can be discussed where appropriate.

Coaching is private within policy limits. Read the confidentiality answer below and the ADHD Coaching Policy for arrangements and booking terms.

Coaching offers practical support for everyday life. It is not therapy or diagnosis. More about coaching and clinical support.

Common questions

Before you get started

Do I need an ADHD diagnosis, or can I enquire while awaiting assessment?

A formal ADHD diagnosis is not required to enquire. You are welcome while awaiting assessment or exploring whether ADHD may be relevant. We can discuss suitability in the free introduction; coaching does not diagnose ADHD.

What is the difference between ADHD coaching and therapy?

Coaching focuses on practical strategies for everyday life. It is not therapy, counselling, crisis support, medical treatment, medical advice, medication management or diagnostic assessment. It is not a substitute for clinical assessment. If you need support outside coaching, we can discuss appropriate signposting.

How often would I need sessions?

There is no fixed frequency. We agree a workable rhythm around your goals and circumstances before paid coaching begins, and can review it as your needs change.

Do I need to buy a package?

For self-funded coaching, you can start with a single session. Six-session and twelve-session packages are optional. Access to Work sessions follow your approved funding arrangements at £110 per 60-minute session.

Is coaching confidential?

Coaching is private, with exceptions for a serious risk of harm, a safeguarding concern, a legal requirement or a need agreed by you. These limits are explained at the start. Internal notes and relevant service records are kept securely. See the Privacy Policy and Safeguarding Policy.
