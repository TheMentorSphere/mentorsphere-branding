1. Introduction and scope

This policy applies across The MentorSphere, including:

  • Subject tutoring through The Maths Mentor, The English Mentor and The Science Mentor
  • ADHD and executive function coaching through The ADHD Mentor
  • Education and SEND support through The Support Mentor

The MentorSphere is founded on inclusive, flexible and neurodiversity-affirming education and mentoring. Different learning needs, communication styles and cognitive profiles are recognised as part of human diversity. Support is developed in partnership with learners and families.

2. Legal and professional framework

The policy is informed by relevant UK legislation and guidance, including:

  • Children and Families Act 2014
  • SEND Code of Practice: 0 to 25 years
  • Equality Act 2010
  • Mental Health Act 1983, as amended
  • Keeping Children Safe in Education
  • Data Protection Act 2018 and UK GDPR

3. Guiding values

  • Accessibility: services can be adapted around sensory, cognitive, communication and emotional needs.
  • Personalisation: each programme is shaped around strengths, goals and circumstances.
  • Neuro-affirming practice: neurodiversity is treated as natural human variation, without minimising real disability or support needs.
  • Emotional safety: sessions aim to be calm, respectful, predictable and validating.
  • Empowerment: learners and families are supported to understand and advocate for their needs.
  • Lived experience: neurodivergent-led insight informs practice alongside professional experience and boundaries.

4. Identifying needs and creating support plans

Information may be gathered through:

  • An initial background questionnaire
  • A baseline or diagnostic learning assessment appropriate to the subject or support goal
  • A free introductory session covering learning, communication, cognitive, sensory and emotional needs
  • Relevant reports, school information or existing support plans supplied with consent
  • Observation of how the learner responds to activities, questions and the online environment

A learner or client profile may bring together:

  • Strengths and areas of interest
  • Barriers and current concerns
  • Learning, coaching and communication preferences
  • Relevant diagnoses, history and lived experience
  • Suggested adaptations and initial strategies

5. Types of support

Individualised support may include:

  • Visual scaffolding, whiteboard interaction and flexible pacing
  • ADHD and executive function strategies
  • Confidence-building, anxiety-sensitive teaching and mindset support
  • Sensory or environmental adaptations
  • Revision plans, study timetables and task breakdown
  • Parent strategy sessions and agreed communication
  • EHCP-related guidance, letter support and education planning
  • Private exam coordination and access support where available

6. Recording and review

  • Tutoring is normally delivered through Lessonspace, with whiteboards saved for continuity.
  • Coaching and support sessions are documented through secure internal notes.
  • Written summaries are shared according to the service agreement and level of detail required.
  • Progress and support strategies are reviewed at agreed checkpoints or when needs change.
  • Consent is obtained before information is shared externally, except where safeguarding or legal duties require otherwise.

7. Safeguarding and mental health

The MentorSphere follows a safeguarding-first and trauma-aware approach. The Safeguarding Policy applies at all times.

  • Designated Safeguarding Lead: Luke Turner
  • Email: luke@thementorsphere.co.uk
  • Telephone: 07955 723 133
  • Mental health concerns are responded to sensitively through reasonable adaptations and appropriate signposting.
  • The MentorSphere does not replace crisis, medical or clinical mental health services.

8. Roles and responsibilities

The policy owner and SEND/SEMH lead is Luke Turner. Any tutor or temporary staff member delivering under The MentorSphere must follow the same core standards, receive relevant learner information and protect confidentiality.

9. Feedback and concerns

Concerns relating to accessibility, SEND provision or neurodivergent inclusion should be raised directly with Luke Turner. Where independent advice is needed, families may also choose to contact services such as SENDIASS or IPSEA.

10. Review

This policy is reviewed annually, and earlier where legislation, best practice, service delivery or learner needs require change.
