# Legitimate interests assessment: ADHD intake ordinary child and third-party information

Version: 1.0

Assessment date: 25 September 2026

Controller and owner: Luke Turner, The MentorSphere

Status: proposed current conclusion for owner adoption. The form is live; this is not a retrospective claim of pre-launch approval. Operational confirmations and risk acceptance remain pending.

## 1. Deliberately limited scope

This LIA covers ordinary child/third-party information that is proportionately necessary to understand a requested coaching/support enquiry. It does **not** assign legitimate interests to every ADHD field.

| Route/data class | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Adult respondent's identity, contact and minimum request information for their own possible contract | Article 6(1)(b) where necessary; outside this child/third-party LIA. |
| Child or combined route: child's name, completed-years age and education stage | Article 6(1)(f) for necessary ordinary enquiry/suitability planning, assessed below. |
| Parent route: parent's own necessary request and support-planning information | Article 6(1)(b), not automatically Article 6(1)(f). |
| Limited ordinary household information about an identifiable other person | Article 6(1)(f) only if needed for the actual support request; general non-identifying household description is preferable. |
| Optional ordinary context solely for additional personalisation | Article 6(1)(a), with appropriate notice and valid affirmative choice; outside this LIA. |
| Optional adult/child health, diagnosis/status, disability, SEND, neurodiversity or health-revealing narrative | Article 6(1)(a) plus Article 9(2)(a); outside this LIA. Parent-own consent does not authorise another person's sensitive information. |
| Unnecessary third-party detail or sensitive content without the valid subject-specific route | Do not use for personalisation; remove/redact and seek the appropriate route if needed. This LIA cannot cure missing consent. |

## 2. Purpose test

The legitimate interest is preparing an informed human response to a request for child/young-person or parent support, checking the requested service is within scope, and avoiding confusion about whom the request concerns. The person/family benefits from relevant adjustments and practical planning without having to submit a medical dossier. The MentorSphere benefits from understanding the request and avoiding unsuitable assumptions.

The form is optional and cannot be required simply to request an introduction. These purposes are lawful and specific; they do not include diagnosis, clinical or safeguarding scoring, advertising, employer monitoring, family profiling or guaranteed access to funding/support. Selecting an ADHD coaching route must not be treated as proof of a diagnosis.

## 3. Necessity test

| Ordinary data | Why and how far necessary | Less intrusive option/limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Child name on child/combined route | Identify the young person about whom direct coaching is requested and distinguish family members | No identity document, address or full date of birth. Do not reproduce the name in notification email. |
| Child completed-years age | Check the deployed direct-coaching boundary of 10 to 17 and support an age-appropriate initial conversation | Age in whole years achieves the purpose without full date of birth; it does not establish consent capacity. |
| Education stage and short Other stage text | Understand relevant daily setting and the context of requested direct support | No school name, report or diagnosis required; ordinary Other prompt warns against health information. |
| Limited household relationships/context about others | May be needed to discuss practical routines or communication affecting the parent's support request | Parent route uses broad household categories, requests no child's name/diagnosis and warns against others' identifiers/health details. Retain actual third-party details only where demonstrably necessary; otherwise remove them. |
| Relevant ordinary authority/relationship context | Understand who is asking and whether it is appropriate to follow up | Required acknowledgement is not evidence that any sensitive consent or legal authority is valid. Ask proportionate follow-up only where needed. |

A direct conversation remains an alternative. For a basic discovery call, little information is needed; the broader optional context is not made contractually or legitimately necessary simply because a structured form offers it. Requests involving another person require a data-subject-specific assessment. A respondent cannot make irrelevant household details necessary by volunteering them.

The parent-only route has no child's identity or age field. Its available household choices describe family structure and support load, not named people's diagnoses. Ordinary third-party data are therefore not an inevitable parent-route requirement. The LIA authorises only relevant incidental ordinary data, not collection of a parallel child record under parent-own consent.

## 4. Balancing test

### Expectations, vulnerability and impact

An adult choosing the child/combined route can reasonably expect a private discussion about requested support. The young person may reasonably expect to be involved and may not expect a parent to disclose personal difficulties or family conflict. Parent-only intake can also reveal context about people who did not participate.

Possible harms include unwanted disclosure, embarrassment, parent/child conflict, loss of control, inaccurate assumptions and reluctance to seek support. These harms may be amplified by age, dependence, neurodivergence or a power difference, without implying that ADHD removes capacity. Children's interests carry additional weight; the adult's wish for support does not automatically outweigh them.

### Safeguards that change the balance

- Ordinary and sensitive purposes remain separate. Article 6(1)(f) does not permit collection of unconsented diagnoses or health narratives.
- The form can be skipped, and ordinary intake can be submitted without the optional sensitive context.
- Child sensitive questions require all three controls: explicit consent, parental responsibility/documented legal authority and learner consent route. A capable learner's authorisation is not replaced by parental preference.
- The parent route asks only about the respondent's experience, with warnings against identifying or health information about others. Combined adult and child consents are separate.
- Route changes clear irrelevant branches and Additional Information; server and receiver reject out-of-route fields. These controls reduce accidental over-sharing but cannot understand all free-text meaning.
- Luke reviews the actual entry, removes irrelevant detail, checks inaccuracies and pauses disputed optional use. He provides child-appropriate privacy information and respects objections.
- Restricted Google storage, authenticated transfer, formula protection, answer-free notifications and diagnostic allowlists reduce dissemination and misuse.
- Six-month prospective retention and consent/withdrawal records support limited use, subject to owner-managed review and deletion.

| Main risk | Residual assessment with safeguards |
| --- | --- |
| Young person's interests are eclipsed by parent's account | Medium: active involvement and meaningful privacy explanation remain essential. |
| Parent-only route discloses another person's private context | Medium: review actual content, prefer general descriptions, remove anything unnecessary or outside valid consent. |
| Inaccurate stage/age/context affects planning | Low: discuss and correct; no clinical or wider suitability scoring. |
| Unauthorised access, family disclosure or retained records | Medium: access/retention controls must be confirmed operationally and maintained. |

The interest can outweigh the limited intrusion for necessary ordinary child/third-party data under these controls. If the learner objects, authority is doubtful or an entry exceeds the relevant purpose, a fresh individual balance is required. This LIA does not support continuing collection/use merely because an adult wants it.

## 5. Objections and practical response

Explain the right to object clearly and separately, including to the young person in a way they understand. Accept verbal/written requests and properly authorised representatives; a parent's original submission does not prevent a capable young person exercising their own rights.

Stop the disputed legitimate-interest processing unless compelling grounds overriding that person's interests, rights and freedoms, or a legal-claims need, can be demonstrated. Investigate their particular concern; the ordinary commercial benefit of completing intake is not itself a compelling ground. Restrict disputed use while resolving the issue where appropriate, consider correction/erasure, and respond within the applicable period, normally one month. Record reasons and complaint routes if refusing. A separate sensitive-consent withdrawal must be acted on without demanding justification or switching basis to continue personalisation.

## 6. Outcome and owner actions

Proposed outcome: **limited reliance on Article 6(1)(f) is justified for necessary ordinary child/third-party data, not all ADHD intake fields**. Adult-own pre-contract information and optional consented context remain under their separate bases. No high residual risk is identified on the described design, conditional on the safeguards operating.

Owner adoption requires confirmation of actual Sheet/folder access, deployed receiver/source match, manual retention/withdrawal operation, age-appropriate transparency and authority review. Check the prior records before applying a clarified basis; do not relabel historic consent evidence. The DPIA records mixed-person narrative and parent-route limitations requiring ongoing review.

No owner approval is recorded. Owner decision/date: **pending**. Review at least annually and immediately when recipients, purposes, fields, age/service scope, consent, retention or automated decision-making change, or a complaint/incident challenges this balance.
