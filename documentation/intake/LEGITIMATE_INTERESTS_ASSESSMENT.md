# Draft legitimate interests assessment: primary learner profile

Status: internal draft for owner approval before processing begins.

## Purpose test

The MentorSphere has a legitimate interest in responding to requests for personalised education support, assessing whether the service is suitable and planning a proportionate introductory discussion. The learner and respondent benefit from avoiding unsuitable or generic support and from not having to repeat relevant ordinary educational information unnecessarily.

The interest does not include marketing, profiling unrelated to the requested service, automated eligibility decisions or collecting information merely because it might be useful later.

## Necessity test

The ordinary learner information requested is directly connected to understanding the requested education support. The form separates optional detail, discourages complete records and identity documents, limits field lengths and assesses subject knowledge separately. A less intrusive route remains available through direct contact or an alternative format.

Only information relevant to assessing and planning support should be used. Optional special-category information is not covered by this assessment alone and requires a separate Article 9 condition.

## Balancing test

Relevant factors supporting the processing:

- The respondent deliberately uses a direct, unlisted form for a stated support-planning purpose.
- The form gives just-in-time privacy information and links to the Privacy Policy.
- The use is limited to suitability, communication and requested support planning.
- Data is sent to a restricted private Sheet and is not used for automated decisions.
- Answers are excluded from notifications, URLs, browser storage, analytics and intentional application logs.
- Prospective records have a six-month review rule and unnecessary submissions are removed promptly.

Factors increasing risk:

- The learner is likely to be a child and may not have completed the form.
- Educational background can be private even when it is not special-category data.
- A parent, carer, professional or other third party may submit information about the learner.
- Optional text may include unexpected sensitive information.

Safeguards:

- Give children's interests particular weight.
- Collect only relevant information and retain all sensitive questions as optional.
- Require the respondent to confirm authority to provide the learner's information.
- Provide age-appropriate privacy information to the learner where practicable.
- Apply separate explicit consent and authority controls before accepting special-category information.
- Restrict Sheet access, use HMAC authentication and retain the existing validation and anti-abuse controls.
- Honour objection and erasure requests subject to applicable legal and safeguarding exceptions.
- Review the assessment if fields, purposes, recipients or retention arrangements change.

## Provisional conclusion

Subject to the safeguards above, Article 6(1)(f) is a proportionate candidate basis for ordinary learner and third-party intake information needed to assess and plan requested support. Owner approval is required before relying on this assessment. It does not provide an Article 9 condition for health, disability, SEND or neurodiversity information.
